Research

New in 2026

  • Provenance Is Enough: Removing Backdoors From the Parameters an Untrusted Source Shaped
    K. I. Ouattara et al. Submitted to IEEE SaTML, 2026 (under review). PDF
    Attributes every parameter of a trained network to the training sources that shaped it, weighted by their share of its gradient, and removes the parameters an untrusted source formed. On MNIST this drives a patch backdoor's attack success from 99.08% to 0.10% for 0.15 points of clean accuracy, with no retraining, no clean reference set and no knowledge of the trigger. Whether the defence applies is decidable before training from the trusted data alone, and it still works at a 0.97% poisoning rate where activation clustering and spectral signatures fail.
  • Position: Learned Aleatoric Uncertainty Should Never Be Reported Without a Measured Noise Floor
    K. I. Ouattara. Preprint, September 2026. PDF
    Argues that a learned aleatoric estimate is the true noise floor plus the model's own error, of unknown magnitude and (in classification) unknown sign, and should be reported against a separately measured floor with the signed gap. Gives an exact decomposition of that gap for regression and classification, measures it on CIFAR-10H against 511,000 human judgements, and reports a pre-registered survey of 50 recent papers, of which 5 compare their estimate to any real floor.
